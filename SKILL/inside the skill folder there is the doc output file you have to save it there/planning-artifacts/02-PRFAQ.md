# FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

## Customer FAQs (Candidate Side)

**Q: I already have LinkedIn badges and course certificates. Why do I need this?**  
A: Certificates prove you completed a course. SkillForge AI proves you can apply that knowledge under job-realistic conditions. A simulation graded by explainable AI — showing your decision-making, problem-solving, and communication across specific dimensions — gives recruiters a signal that a certificate cannot.

**Q: How is this different from a generic online test?**  
A: Generic MCQ tests have two problems: they are easy to cheat, and they measure memorization, not ability. SkillForge AI's career simulations put you in realistic job scenarios — handling ambiguous tasks, making decisions under constraints, communicating your reasoning — then grade your performance the same way a skilled manager would evaluate a real employee.

**Q: Will recruiters actually look at my SkillForge profile?**  
A: Recruiters on the platform have access to your verified assessment and simulation scores as part of every job match. The data is structured, explainable, and directly tied to the requirements of each job — which is more than a resume keyword filter can offer.

**Q: What careers are supported?**  
A: At launch, SkillForge AI supports [N] career tracks. Each track has its own assessment question set, simulation scenarios, and skill-gap framework, maintained and updated by our content team.

**Q: Is my data private?**  
A: Your performance data is yours. You control whether and how recruiters can see your profile. We never use your data to train AI models without explicit consent.

## Customer FAQs (HR / Recruiter Side)

**Q: How is SkillForge AI different from an ATS?**  
A: An ATS manages the application workflow. SkillForge AI gives you the evidence to make better decisions inside that workflow — by replacing resume-keyword guessing with demonstrated-skill scores, and replacing opaque match algorithms with per-dimension explanations you can defend to a hiring manager.

**Q: Can I trust the AI match score?**  
A: Every match score includes a full breakdown — which dimensions the candidate scored well on, which are weak, and which job requirements they do not yet meet. You are never asked to trust a number without understanding why it was generated.

**Q: Does the AI make hiring decisions for me?**  
A: No. SkillForge AI provides evidence and recommendations. Every final hiring decision is made by a human. The system will never move a candidate to "Hired" without a recruiter or hiring manager taking that action.

**Q: How do I define job requirements?**  
A: You create a structured job posting that specifies required skills, preferred skills, experience level, and assessment criteria. The AI matching engine uses this structured data — not free-text keywords — to score candidates against your requirements.

**Q: What if a candidate games the assessment?**  
A: Our assessment and simulation design makes gaming significantly harder than a simple MCQ quiz — scenario-based responses, response-time analysis, and re-assessment cooldowns all add friction against gaming. We are transparent that perfect anti-cheating is not claimed at launch, and we are building toward stronger verification mechanisms over time.

## Stakeholder FAQs (Internal / Investor)

**Q: What is the core business hypothesis?**  
A: More candidate assessments generate richer, structured skill data. Richer skill data makes recruiter matching more valuable. Higher recruiter value drives paid HR adoption. This network effect is the foundation of the Freemium model — but it is a hypothesis to be validated, not an assumed outcome.

**Q: What is the primary revenue source?**  
A: The HR/recruiter subscription tier. The candidate side is free with usage limits, designed to grow the skill-data asset that powers recruiter value.

**Q: What is the biggest risk?**  
A: Two risks at launch: (1) AI grading accuracy — addressed through rubric-anchored scoring, confidence indicators, and human override paths; (2) the two-sided cold-start problem — addressed by proving the closed candidate loop first (MVP), before over-investing in breadth.
